Rather than relying on a single centralized agency, Central Florida integrates nonprofit accelerators, university-backed business programs, government-assisted consulting centers, technical community groups, and specialized maker spaces.

### 1. Dedicated Nonprofit Accelerators & Incubator Programs

### Nonprofit and community-run accelerators guide startups from initial concept validation through pre-seed scaling:
* **Building Our Tech (BOT):** A 501(c)(3) organization focused on accelerating economic mobility through technology education and founder development. Its Entrepreneur & Startup Education track features the *Bridge to Launchpad Incubator* and *Launchpad Accelerator*, alongside pitch competitions. Over 10 years, BOT has engaged 10,000+ tech professionals, trained 100+ founders, and supported 57 companies—with BOT-backed founders raising over $20 million. [BOT](https://buildingourtech.org/) was also named a recipient of a $15,000 City of Orlando / Orlando Economic Partnership Tech Grant to fund its AI-Enabled Cloud Talent Accelerator.  We are thankful that BOT continues to support our yearly DevFest Florida conference.
* **UCF Business Incubation Program:** Established in 1999, this university system provides pre-incubation, incubation, international "Soft Landing" programs, and specialized veterans entrepreneurship initiatives. It operates facilities across Central Florida, including Apopka, Kissimmee, Lake Nona, Winter Springs, and the Central Florida Research Park.
* **StarterStudio:** Central Florida’s primary 501(c)(3) nonprofit focused strictly on accelerating tech startups. It offers structured Idea Stage, Build Stage, and Pre-Seed Stage accelerator cohorts, providing mentorship, legal and financial advisory, educational programming, and investor connections. Under executive leadership including CEO Dawn Haynes and Programming Director Bob Reed, StarterStudio has worked with nearly 410 tech ventures over roughly a decade. It also ties directly into early capital via the FireSpring Fund seed fund.

### 2. One-on-One Business Advising & Institutional Support

### For general business strategy, financial planning, and operational coaching, regional institutions provide free and low-cost professional guidance:
* **Florida SBDC at UCF:** Located within the National Entrepreneur Center (NEC), the Small Business Development Center offers no-cost, confidential 1-on-1 business consulting, risk management advisory, and market research. It also manages advanced programs like the Advisory Board Council, CEO XChange, and the Florida APEX Accelerator for government contracting readiness.
* **SCORE Orlando:** Driven by experienced volunteer business leaders, SCORE provides free confidential mentoring, online learning modules (such as the *Startup Roadmap*), and interactive webinars across Orange, Seminole, Osceola, and Brevard counties.
* **Academic Feeder Programs:** Additional university-affiliated resources include Blackstone LaunchPad (student entrepreneurial coaching), UCF I-Corps (product feasibility and commercialization), and the UCF Venture Lab & Accelerator.
* **National Entrepreneur Center (NEC):** Functions as a centralized hub hosting multiple support agencies under one roof to simplify resource discovery for business owners.

### 3. Technical Upskilling, Community Networks & Specialized Maker Hubs

### To support technology ventures, Orlando provides both technical workforce training and specialized physical equipment:
 * **Melrose Center (located at the downtown Orlando Public Library):** A 26,000 sq. ft. facility offering a 3D printing fabrication lab, audio/video production studios, photography bays, and immersive simulators.
 * [MakerFX Makerspace](https://www.makerfx.org/) is a community makerspace in South Orlando. We welcome makers of all types, from crafters to artists to robotics builders. Whatever you like to make, you'll find a welcoming community that can help you learn, and wants to learn from you. We are active in the local community and you'll often find MakerFX members showing off what they make at events, volunteering with community projects, partnering with organizations like Boys & Girls Clubs of Central Florida, and much, much more.  We very much appreciate their leadership for Orlando [MakerFaire](https://www.makerfaireorlando.com/), a celebration of open innovation, DIY, and local technology artists.
 * **FamiLab:** Community makerspaces/hackerspaces equipped for 3D printing, laser cutting, woodworking, metalworking, and rapid prototyping.  Check out their awesome event calendar: [FamiLab events](https://www.familab.org/common-public-events)
 * [HiveLabs](https://www.hivelabs.community/who-we-are) is a nonprofit adult makerspace in downtown Lake Nona. We were built on a simple idea: when people have access to the right tools and mentorship, they can make anything.
 * **GuideWell Innovation Center (Lake Nona):** Focuses on health-tech acceleration, medical device innovation, and health solutions.
 * **Orlando Game Space & NeoWare Studios:** Specialty hubs tailored to digital media, gaming, and interactive content creators.

### **Overview of Investment Groups & Capital Sources**
Orlando’s capital landscape features a diverse network of venture capital firms, angel investor groups, private equity funds, and hybrid platforms:
* **Venture Capital & Early-Stage Funds**: Key early-stage VC firms active in the region include **[Deepwork Capital](https://www.deepworkcapital.com/)** (focused on early-stage company building), **[venVelo](https://venvelo.com/)** (an early-stage VC fund offering capital and mentorship), **[Kirenaga Partners](https://kirenaga.com/)**, and **[IDEA Fund Partners](https://www.ideafundpartners.com/)**. Additionally, **[Florida Funders](https://www.floridafunders.com/)** operates as a hybrid venture capital fund and crowdfunding platform targeting early-stage Florida tech startups.
* **Angel Networks & Crowdfunding**: The **Florida Angel Nexus** powers angel investment groups statewide by coordinating deal flow and organizing investors, while local networks like **Winter Park Angels** provide equity funding to area entrepreneurs. Startup financing is also accessible via equity crowdfunding through **Merging Traffic**, which matches accredited investors with founders.
* **Opportunity Funds & Growth Equity**: **Orlando Opportunity Fund** is a private fund focused on converting unrealized capital gains into local Central Florida investments. **Arsenal Venture Partners** focuses on growth equity at the intersection of government, large corporations, and emerging tech, while **NewGate Capital Partners** provides private capital through seasoned investors and entrepreneurs.

### **Addressing Seed-Stage Gaps & Alternative Funding Pathways**
The sources emphasize structured mechanisms to bridge early funding gaps and provide non-traditional capital pathways:
* **Dedicated Seed Funds**: **FireSpring Fund** (a nonprofit evergreen seed fund tied to StarterStudio) directly targets the seed funding deficit that often hinders Central Florida tech startups.
* **Grants & Public Financing**: Local public-private partnerships offer non-dilutive assistance, such as the **City of Orlando and Orlando Economic Partnership Tech Grants** (e.g., Capability Catalyst Grants for technology initiatives).
* **Pitch Competitions & Accelerators**: Programmatic funding opportunities exist across incubators and community initiatives. For instance, **[Building Our Tech (BOT)](https://buildingourtech.org/)** hosts pitch competitions and funding pathways (with BOT-backed founders having raised over **$20 million**), and the **[GuideWell Innovation Center](https://guidewellinnovation.com/)** holds an annual *Health + Accel* pitch competition with cash prizes for health-tech startups.

### **Integration in the Broader Orlando Tech Ecosystem**
Investment groups do not operate in a vacuum; they function within an interconnected network of regional support:
* **Institutional & Economic Leadership**: Regional organizations such as the **[Orlando Economic Partnership](https://orlando.org/)** and the **Orlando Tech Council** work to amplify local success stories and strengthen capital access. Major global innovation platforms like **[Plug and Play](https://www.plugandplaytechcenter.com/)** have also established an Orlando office focusing on Smart Cities.
* **Incubators & Readiness Programs**: Organizations including the **UCF Business Incubation Program**, **UCF Venture Lab**, **Blackstone LaunchPad**, and **FSBDC at UCF** prepare early-stage companies to become investment-ready and navigate capital acquisition.
* **Grassroots Community & Network Events**: Grassroots networks like **StarterStudio**, **[Orlando Devs](https://www.orlandodevs.com/)**, **[Orlando Innovation League](https://orlinnovationleague.org/)**, and **[OrlandoPreneur](https://orlandopreneur.com/)** regularly bring founders, mentors, and investors together at monthly meetup happy hours, pitch nights, and roundtables to accelerate dealmaking and relationship building.

### **Economic Development & Cutting-Edge Industry Research**
OEP maintains a dedicated **Resource Center** that leverages economic and industry data to provide market intelligence for emerging startups, relocating enterprises, and investors:
* **[Data Center & Benchmarking Tools](https://business.orlando.org/l/data-center/)**: OEP offers specialized market research tools, including the **Data Center**, the **Top 30 MSA Comparison Tool**, **Regional Demographics**, and data on **Relocations & Expansions**.
* **[Market Tracking & Surveys](https://orlando.org/l/partnership-reports/)**: The Partnership publishes regular intelligence reports, such as the **Monthly Economic Snapshot**, the **Orlando MSA Market Update**, and the **Orlando MSA Business Conditions Survey**, alongside market commentaries.
* **Emerging Tech Platforms**: OEP utilizes advanced visualization and talent analytics, including the **[Orlando Regional Digital Twin](https://business.orlando.org/l/orlando-regional-digital-twin/)** and **UpSkill Orlando: Skills Explorer**, to help businesses evaluate regional talent pipelines and market conditions.

### **2. Startup Resources, Grants & Growth Programs**
OEP serves as a centralized gateway to financing options, institutional support networks, and targeted innovation grants:
* **Direct Grants & Tech Initiatives**: OEP collaborates directly with municipal leadership on funding programs, such as the **City of Orlando and Orlando Economic Partnership Tech Grants**. For example, a $15,000 *Capability Catalyst Grant* was awarded to **Building Our Tech (BOT)** to fund its AI-Enabled Cloud Talent Accelerator.
* **Financing Pathways**: OEP provides guidance across all business growth phases, pointing founders toward commercial financing, government funding, regional matching grants, venture capital firms, and angel networks.
* **The Orlando Tech Council**: Operating under the OEP umbrella, the **Orlando Tech Council** develops targeted innovation programs to strengthen regional resources, accelerate scaling opportunities for tech ventures, and raise Orlando's global profile.
* **Ecosystem Resource Mapping**: OEP actively connects entrepreneurs with key institutional growth platforms, including:
 * **GrowFL**: Focused on strategies and next-level growth resources for second-stage companies.
 * **National Entrepreneur Center (NEC)**: A public-private cooperative providing accessible business assistance and training.
 * **Florida Virtual Entrepreneur Center**: An online directory mapping resources across every stage of business development.
 * **Academic Partners**: Promoting programs like the **Small Business Development Center (SBDC) at UCF** and the **UCF Business Incubation Program**.
